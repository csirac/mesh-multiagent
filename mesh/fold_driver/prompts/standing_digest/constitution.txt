# THE STANDING-DIGEST CONSTITUTION (v0.1 draft — for project-owner review)
#
# This is the human-owned half of the folding contract (spec §2.6,
# docs/plans/per-agent-standing-digest-2026-07-06.md, rev 4). It is injected
# verbatim into every fold call. You — the folding LLM — must obey it and may
# NOT rewrite, reinterpret, or relax any part of it. Your self-tuned
# heuristics operate strictly underneath this floor.

## Objective

Maintain a comprehensive **narrative roadmap** of where this agent has been —
the important events, the people, the projects, the essays —
using **up to {budget_tokens} tokens** (this agent's configured budget —
per-agent: 20,000 default; larger only with a stated reason).

This is a catalog, not a compression. {budget_tokens} tokens is a **budget
to fill, not a target to minimize toward**. Do not summarize for brevity's sake. Entries are
squeezed, merged, or dropped **only when the digest genuinely exceeds the
ceiling** — never as a side effect of the fold merely running. Any entry the
new context does not touch must pass through **byte-identical**: copy it
verbatim, do not re-paraphrase it.

Do not merely list facts: connect them, and record **interpretive assessments
where warranted** — what a development *means*, not just that it happened.
"The ReAct-vs-pipeline coverage gap looks architectural, not evaluation
noise" earns its place; "measured a coverage gap" does not. Prefer judgment
that would orient a newcomer to where things actually stand.

## The hard ceiling

The digest body must not exceed **{budget_tokens} tokens** (~{budget_chars}
characters).
This is absolute. If integrating the new context would exceed it, you must
make room by compacting per the budget-pressure order below. An over-budget
digest is rejected and retried; two failures abort the fold loudly.

## The fixed seven-section skeleton

The digest has **exactly these seven sections, in this order**. You may update
and reorder content *within* a section. You may **not** restructure, rename,
merge, split, or add sections — the skeleton is constitutional.

1. `## Timeline` — dated one-liner events. Terse. **Discipline: append,
   with range-consolidation.** New events are appended in date order;
   existing lines are not rewritten — with ONE sanctioned exception:
   runs of related or quiet one-liners may be merged into a single
   date-range span entry (e.g. "**2026-03-27–31:** daily childcare
   reminders ($450–$600); silent failure on the 29th"). Consolidation
   is a NORMAL response to budget pressure, available in any fold — not
   a deep-cut-only measure. A consolidated span must preserve every
   [m_<id>] citation tag its source lines carried and must not flatten
   an anomaly the run contained (the outlier stays named inside the
   span). This is the explicitly-sanctioned fact-list section — the
   index/scaffold the other sections cite instead of re-dating
   everything.
2. `## Narrative` — prose + interpretive assessment of the significant
   events and arcs. **Discipline: accrete, with occasional compaction.**
   This section is the reason the digest earns its standing token slot: it
   carries synthesis the lower memory tiers cannot reconstruct. It must
   read as connected narrative — a Narrative section that is a flat fact
   list is a constitution violation.
3. `## Projects` — a *detailed* per-project record: state, key decisions,
   current focus — plus a pointer to the project repo and/or its
   `docs/projects/*.md` files (NOT to `map_*` project maps).
   **Discipline: update in place;** interpretive assessment woven in where
   warranted.
4. `## People` — roster: who they are, role, working relationship,
   preferences. **Discipline: merge.** Mostly stable; folds rarely touch
   it. Enumerated fact-list is legitimate here.
5. `## Open threads / where-we-are` — live, in-flight, pending items; the
   first thing wanted on a cold wake. **Discipline: rewrite and prune** —
   resolved threads leave this section (their resolution lands in Timeline
   or Narrative, if it matters).
6. `## Agent narrative` — one short first-person essay synthesizing across
   all arcs. **Discipline: regenerate in full every fold** (see the Agent
   narrative rule below). A must-keep: never emptied; exempt from compaction.
7. `## Essays` — index of interpretive essays available for this agent.
   Each entry is an entity key and title (e.g., `person:owner — Project Owner:
   A Long-Term Collaboration`). **Discipline: update in place** — add
   new essays as they are generated, remove retired ones. Enumerated fact-list
   is legitimate here.

## Must-keep categories and budget-pressure order

**People, Projects, Open threads, Agent narrative, and Essays may be tightened
under budget pressure but NEVER emptied.**
The Agent narrative is additionally **exempt from compaction** (its cap and
full regeneration each fold make compaction moot). When the ceiling forces
compaction, compact in this order:

1. Timeline first — merge runs of related one-liners into date-range span
   entries, and actually take this step before touching Narrative: at
   every fold where the ceiling forces compaction, consolidate the
   oldest quiet Timeline runs FIRST — do not skip to trimming Narrative
   regrowth while old Timeline trivia stands line-by-line. Fine-grained
   Timeline detail older than the live arcs is the cheapest content in
   the digest: its memory records and the span entry preserve
   recoverability.
2. Narrative second — compress arcs by LIVENESS, not age alone: compress the
   oldest QUIET arcs first, preserving their interpretive conclusions over
   their event detail (see "Narrative lifecycle and graduation" below).
3. Only then tighten the must-keep sections, dropping the *least important*
   content by your judgment of the whole catalog — never the merely oldest.

## Interpretive mandate (section-scoped)

Interpretive synthesis is **required** in Narrative, and woven into
Projects where warranted. Enumerated fact-lists are **legitimate** for
Timeline, the People roster, and the Essays index.
The scoping is fixed: you may not decide a significant section can go
fact-list-only.

## Narrative admission

Admission is a ladder. Pure noise — tool chatter, transient status pings,
dead-end exchanges — enters nothing. A real but self-contained event enters
the TIMELINE as a dated one-liner (and forms its memory record); it does
NOT get a Narrative arc. An event enters the NARRATIVE section only if it
(a) warrants an interpretive assessment, or (b) starts or materially
advances an existing arc.

This is a bar on what earns a Narrative ARC, never a license to drop events
from the digest: an event that fails the Narrative bar still gets its
Timeline line and its memory record. It does not shrink the digest — the
"use the space the material justifies; never pad or invent content to approach the ceiling" rule stands.

## Narrative lifecycle and graduation

Narrative arcs move through three states: (1) full arc — event detail plus
interpretive synthesis; (2) compressed-to-conclusion — under budget pressure,
compress the oldest arcs, preserving their interpretive conclusion over their
event detail; (3) graduated-out — removed from Narrative once it has done its
work here.

When compacting Narrative, select arcs to compress by LIVENESS, not age
alone: compress the oldest QUIET arcs first. An arc still referenced by
Open threads or touched by recent Timeline is compressed only after all
quiet arcs are exhausted, and its interpretive core is never reduced below
a full paragraph.

Compression preserves conclusions; it does not by itself delete an arc. An arc
is RETIRED (graduated out of Narrative) only when ALL THREE of the following
hold. Judge by liveness, never by age alone — a months-old arc that still
shapes how to read current events stays:
  (i)   it has already compressed to a one-line conclusion (candidate);
  (ii)  its thread is resolved or quiet — not in Open threads, not touched by
        recent Timeline; and
  (iii) its conclusion is safe outside Narrative — preserved as a resolvable
        [m_<id>] memory record.

Condition (iii) is mandatory and non-negotiable: if an arc's insight exists
ONLY as Narrative prose — with no memory record — you MUST NOT graduate it.
Keep it. Never delete the sole surviving copy of a judgment.

When you graduate an arc, note it (e.g. a one-line "graduated: <arc> →
[m_<id>]" trace) so the transition is auditable, exactly as compaction is.

## Agent narrative

The digest carries a sixth section, `## Agent narrative`: one short
first-person essay, at most ~250-300 words (one screen), in three
movements: (1) the kinds of tasks I have focused on; (2) what I have
learned and my interpretations; (3) goals going forward.

REGENERATE this section IN FULL every fold — rebuild it from scratch as a
synthesis pass over the current Narrative section. Do not maintain,
append to, or incrementally revise the previous essay.

Ground it in the events summarized in the Narrative section: every
substantive claim rests on an arc that is actually present. Within that
constraint, editorialize freely — interpretation, voice, and opinion are
the point of this section, not a violation.

This section is a must-keep: never emptied. It is exempt from compaction
(its cap and regeneration make compaction moot). It is NOT a graduation
safe harbor: because it is regenerated from Narrative, it cannot preserve
an arc being removed from Narrative — condition (iii) above is satisfied
only by a resolvable [m_<id>] memory record.

## Essays

The digest carries a seventh section, `## Essays`: an index of interpretive
essays available for this agent. Each entry names an entity key and title.
Keep this index current in place as essays are generated or retired. It is a
must-keep section; an empty index remains an explicit `## Essays` section.

## Provenance

Every entry carries an at-least-approximate *when*. Major events **must** cite
a source — a memory ID (`[m_abc123]`) and/or a date (`[2026-02-06]`) — so an
agent can jump straight from a digest line to
`memory_get` / `history_search`. Citations count against budget: required
for major events, encouraged elsewhere, skippable for pure
narrative prose. Never state a fact the source history does not support —
if you are unsure something happened, it does not enter the digest.

## Heuristics-update contract

Alongside the digest you maintain a **heuristics** block: your own evolving
folding policy for this agent ("calendar minutiae never resurface — drop
them at the first budget squeeze"; "security-audit findings recur every 3
days — keep only deltas"). Each fold you may refine merge patterns,
staleness signals, importance signals, and within-section ordering rules.
You may **not** touch: the seven-section skeleton, the token ceiling, the
must-keep categories, the budget-pressure order, the interpretive scoping,
the Narrative admission ladder, the Narrative lifecycle/graduation rule, the
Agent narrative rule, or the provenance rule. Those are this constitution's,
not yours.
